# Name the method before doing arithmetic

These **12 original retrieval prompts** are additional to the 76 source prompts and 29-question Core path. Hide the solutions, identify the unknown and percentage base, then calculate. They are not PYQs.

<details>
<summary>R1. A ₹40 share is available at ₹8 premium. What value belongs in the investment formula?</summary>

M = 40 + 8 = **₹48**. Rupee premium, not 8% premium.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R2. ₹6,000 buys ₹50 shares at ₹60 paying 10%. Find annual income.</summary>

n = 6000/60 = 100; d = 50 × .10 = ₹5; D = **₹500**. This is the funded-income chain.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R3. ₹100 shares pay 8%. How many are required for ₹1,200 annual income?</summary>

d = ₹8; n = 1200/8 = **150 shares**. Market price is unnecessary until investment is requested.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R4. An investor gets ₹300 from 200 ₹25 shares. Find the declared rate.</summary>

r = 300/(200 × 25) × 100 = **6%**. Denominator is total face value.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R5. A 12% ₹100 share yields 8%. Find its price.</summary>

M = 100 × 12/8 = **₹150**. Reverse yield, not dividend-rate calculation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R6. A ₹50 share costs ₹40 and yields 10%. Find the company dividend rate.</summary>

r = 10 × 40/50 = **8%**. The question asks the company’s rate, not the investor’s return.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R7. Current income is ₹360. Each share earns ₹6. How many extra shares bring income TO ₹600?</summary>

Extra income = 600 − 360 = ₹240; extra shares = 240/6 = **40**. “To” differs from “by.”

</details>

<details>
<summary>R8. You hold 80 shares bought at ₹50. Sell 20 at ₹70. What is capital gain?</summary>

20 × (70 − 50) = **₹400**. Compare only the sold shares’ cost with their sale proceeds.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R9. One old share sells for ₹60 and earned ₹3 annually. New shares cost ₹20 and earn ₹2 each. Income increases by ₹450. Find old shares sold.</summary>

One old share buys three new shares, earning ₹6. Increase = ₹3 per old share; old count = 450/3 = **150**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R10. Equal amounts earn 8% and 12%; combined income is ₹1,000. Find total capital.</summary>

Let each amount be x. .08x + .12x = 1000; x = ₹5,000, so **total = ₹10,000**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R11. Annual income rises from ₹800 to ₹1,000 on original capital ₹10,000. Compare growth in income with change relative to original capital.</summary>

Income growth = 200/800 × 100 = **25%**. Extra income/original capital = 200/10000 × 100 = **2%**; income-to-original-capital ratio rises by **2 percentage points**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>R12. ₹5,000 is spent on ₹100 shares paying 10%. Can you calculate the dividend uniquely?</summary>

**No.** Market price is missing. At par it would be ₹500; at ₹125 it would be ₹400. Do not silently assume at par.

</details>

## Source issues to recognise

| Source | Issue | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| W04, p. 26 | Calls total face value “investment” | Correct the label; 8% dividend-rate answer remains valid |
| W12, pp. 30–31 | “Percentage increase in return” | Use its explicit original-capital denominator; distinguish relative income growth |
| T01d, p. 34 | Buying price absent | No unique dividend; state a conditional at-par answer only |
| T01h, pp. 34–35 | Investor dividend called total company dividend | Separate intended investor reading from literal whole-company claim |
| T01i, p. 35 | “Rate” not defined as fraction or numerical percentage | State .10 / 10% versus numerical 10 consistently |
| T01j, p. 35 | B is printed at ₹1620 | Preserve the printed figure rather than guessing a correction |
| T03, p. 35 | “Shares worth ₹72,000” ambiguous | Declare the nominal-value assumption; inspect the alternative |
| T12(ii), p. 35 | Calls market-priced shares “₹125 shares” | Stem supplies F = ₹100 and M = ₹125 |

A student should not lose confidence because a source question is underspecified. The task is to distinguish a missing datum from a missing skill.

## What to write in an exam answer

Give the relevant formula, substitute values with the right base, show the share-count or equation step, and label the final unit. For income change, explicitly say **increase** or **decrease**. For comparisons, state both yields and your conclusion.
