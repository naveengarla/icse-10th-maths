## What “redundant” means here

A source question classified **Redundant** adds no uncovered variation to the selected learning path. It remains available for Strengthen or Exam. The matrix gives a **Core witness for every variation of every subpart**. Coverage may be distributed across several Core questions rather than a single identical question.

**Representative** means selected for Core or as one of the 13 worked examples. **Special** flags an unusual format, a difficult boundary case, or a source issue. These labels are mutually exclusive; Special takes precedence, while Core selection is a separate Yes/No field. A special question can still be in Core.

## Compression that can be checked

- The Core path uses **25 source prompts plus 4 original transfer prompts**, containing **52 subparts**. The original prompts are P01–P04 and are not counted as textbook questions.
- **13 selected source worked examples** provide teaching support. Reading them is not equivalent to independent practice.
- The **28-question Exam bank** reuses some Core questions. There are also **12 original mixed-retrieval prompts** on the Recognition page.
- **34 source prompts** are classified redundant for the selected path; **11** are special. The other **31** are representatives. These are source-only classification counts.
- Core selection is inclusion-minimal under the recorded variation tags: deleting any complete Core prompt leaves a gap. This does not prove that 29 is the globally smallest set or that every learner needs exactly 29 questions.
- Compression is measured against **source prompts and subparts**, not misleadingly by comparing 29 multi-part questions with 76 single-part tasks. The audit exposes both granularities.

The independent-practice baseline should expand whenever a learner struggles with a variation. If a future source introduces a new combination requiring a different method, add a new variation rather than forcing it into an existing tag.

## Recognition, methods and common errors

### Quoted market price

**Recognition:** Quoted market price

**Method:** M = quoted price

**Common error:** Do not add the quoted price to face value.

### Premium in rupees

**Recognition:** Premium in rupees

**Method:** M = F + premium

**Common error:** ₹7 premium is not 7% unless F = ₹100.

### Discount in rupees

**Recognition:** Discount in rupees

**Method:** M = F − discount

**Common error:** Subtract from face value, not investment.

### Premium as a percentage

**Recognition:** Premium as a percentage

**Method:** M = F(1 + p/100)

**Common error:** The percentage is of face value.

### Discount as a percentage

**Recognition:** Discount as a percentage

**Method:** M = F(1 − p/100)

**Common error:** 20% discount leaves 80%, not 20%.

### At par

**Recognition:** At par

**Method:** M = F

**Common error:** At par is a condition; never assume it from missing data.

### Shares → investment

**Recognition:** How much money is required?

**Method:** I = nM

**Common error:** Use market value, not face value.

### Investment → shares

**Recognition:** How many shares?

**Method:** n = I/M

**Common error:** Do not divide by dividend per share.

### Shares → annual income

**Recognition:** Annual dividend on n shares

**Method:** d = Fr/100; D = nd

**Common error:** Dividend is calculated on face value.

### Company-wide dividend

**Recognition:** Total dividend paid on all issued shares

**Method:** Dcompany = NFr/100

**Common error:** Use all issued shares, not one investor’s holding.

### Half-yearly → annual dividend

**Recognition:** 10% half-yearly dividend

**Method:** Annual D = 2nF(10/100)

**Common error:** Double only when 10% is for each half-year, not an annual rate paid half-yearly.

### Income → dividend rate

**Recognition:** Rate declared when total dividend is known

**Method:** r = 100D/(nF)

**Common error:** nF is total face value, not necessarily money invested.

### Income → shares

**Recognition:** Shares required for annual income

**Method:** n = D/d

**Common error:** Divide by dividend per share, not price.

### Target income → investment

**Recognition:** Amount needed to earn a stated income

**Method:** n = D/d; I = nM

**Common error:** Do not stop at share count.

### Additional income → extra shares

**Recognition:** Increase income BY an amount

**Method:** Extra n = ΔD/d

**Common error:** BY means additional; TO means target total.

### Additional income → extra investment

**Recognition:** How much extra must be invested?

**Method:** Extra I = (ΔD/d)M

**Common error:** Multiply additional shares by market price.

### Investment and income → price

**Recognition:** Price paid when I and D are known

**Method:** n = D/d; M = I/n

**Common error:** Do not confuse total investment with price per share.

### Income → return on investment

**Recognition:** Percentage income / yield / return

**Method:** y = 100D/I = Fr/M

**Common error:** r and y are numerical percentages, e.g. 12, not 0.12.

### Yield → market price

**Recognition:** Earns y% although dividend is r%

**Method:** M = Fr/y

**Common error:** Dividend and yield have different bases.

### Yield → dividend rate

**Recognition:** What rate is the company paying?

**Method:** r = yM/F

**Common error:** Return is based on market price; dividend on face value.

### Yield versus dividend → premium/discount

**Recognition:** Is the share above or below par?

**Method:** For positive r: y &lt; r means M &gt; F; y &gt; r means M &lt; F

**Common error:** A higher dividend percentage is not automatically a higher yield.

### Nearest-integer yield

**Recognition:** Give return to the nearest integer

**Method:** Calculate exact y, then round once

**Common error:** Do not round intermediate share prices or dividends.

### Choose by yield

**Recognition:** Which is the better investment?

**Method:** Compare F₁r₁/M₁ and F₂r₂/M₂

**Common error:** Compare yield, not dividend rate or dividend per share.

### Known equal investments → income difference

**Recognition:** Difference in annual income on equal sums

**Method:** Difference = I × \|y₁ − y₂\|/100

**Common error:** Do not report percentage points when rupees are asked.

### Partial sale → capital gain

**Recognition:** Sells a fraction of holdings at a new price

**Method:** Gain = nSold(Msell − Mbuy)

**Common error:** Deduct the cost of sold shares, not all original shares.

### Sale → new shares → income

**Recognition:** Invests the sale proceeds

**Method:** S = nOld Msell; nNew = S/Mnew; Dnew = nNew dNew

**Common error:** Share counts do not transfer between companies; money does.

### Reinvestment → change in income

**Recognition:** Find change in annual income

**Method:** ΔD = Dnew − Dold; state increase or decrease

**Common error:** Sale proceeds are not annual income.

### Sale PLUS dividend → reinvestment

**Recognition:** Including dividend

**Method:** New capital = sale proceeds + old dividend

**Common error:** Add dividend only when the question explicitly reinvests it.

### Income change relative to ORIGINAL investment

**Recognition:** Increase in return on original investment

**Method:** 100(Dnew − Dold)/Ioriginal

**Common error:** This differs from relative income growth: 100ΔD/Dold.

### Cash target → shares sold → remaining income

**Recognition:** Sells just enough to raise ₹S

**Method:** nSold = S/Msell; nLeft = nOld − nSold

**Common error:** Cash raised is not capital gain.

### Fraction sold → remaining income

**Recognition:** Income from the remaining 20%

**Method:** nLeft = (1 − sold fraction)nOld; Dleft = nLeft d

**Common error:** Do not pay future dividend on shares already sold.

### Capital gain PLUS dividend

**Recognition:** Profit including dividend

**Method:** Profit = sale proceeds − original cost + dividend

**Common error:** Keep annual dividend separate until the final addition.

### Income increase → old shares

**Recognition:** Unknown number sold; annual income increases

**Method:** n[(Msell/Mnew)dNew − dOld] = increase

**Common error:** The per-old-share change is not dNew − dOld.

### Income decrease → old shares

**Recognition:** Change stated without direction

**Method:** Compute signed per-share change first; use decrease if negative

**Common error:** A negative change coefficient cannot equal a positive increase.

### Income change → original investment

**Recognition:** Find the sum invested originally

**Method:** Solve for old n, then Ioriginal = nMbuy

**Common error:** The original buying price can differ from sale price.

### Two selling prices; ambiguous worth

**Recognition:** Shares worth a stated amount sold separately

**Method:** Define what worth means; price each batch, then subtract original cost

**Common error:** A nominal-value amount and sale proceeds imply different share counts.

### Equal investments/incomes → unknown rate

**Recognition:** Both receive equal dividend

**Method:** I₁d₁/M₁ = I₂F₂r₂/(100M₂)

**Common error:** Equal investment does not imply equal numbers of shares.

### Equal investments; combined income known

**Recognition:** Invests equally; sum of returns

**Method:** x(d₁/M₁ + d₂/M₂) = total D; total I = 2x

**Common error:** Do not report x when the total investment is requested.

### Equal investments; income difference known

**Recognition:** Difference in returns is ₹k

**Method:** x\|d₁/M₁ − d₂/M₂\| = k; total I = 2x

**Common error:** Use difference of yields, not their sum.

### Fixed capital; equal incomes

**Recognition:** Divide a sum into two parts

**Method:** xd₁/M₁ = (T − x)d₂/M₂

**Common error:** Equal incomes do not generally require equal investment.

### Three-company unknown remainder

**Recognition:** Two investments known, total income known

**Method:** D₃ = Dtotal − D₁ − D₂; I₃ = D₃M₃/d₃; y = 100Dtotal/Itotal

**Common error:** Overall yield needs total capital, not the average of the three yields.

### Missing market price

**Recognition:** Investment and face value without buying price

**Method:** State what is missing; show any conditional answer

**Common error:** Do not silently assume at par.

### Assertion–reason / paired statements

**Recognition:** Which statements are true? Does R explain A?

**Method:** Compute each statement independently, then test the logical link

**Common error:** True reason alone does not make the assertion true.

### Discount followed by gain percentage

**Recognition:** Bought at discount; sold at 25% gain

**Method:** Buy = F(1 − discount/100); sell = buy(1 + gain/100)

**Common error:** 20% discount then 25% gain cannot be combined by subtraction.

### Case-study comparison

**Recognition:** Same scenario supplies several subquestions

**Method:** Make one F/M/r table; use it for each subquestion

**Common error:** Use each company’s own face value.

### Investment → shares → annual income

**Recognition:** Capital is given instead of a share count

**Method:** n = I/M, then D = nFr/100

**Common error:** Applying dividend rate directly to the investment ignores market price.

### Different buying prices; combine dividend

**Recognition:** Two batches of the same share bought at different prices

**Method:** Dtotal = (n₁ + n₂)Fr/100

**Common error:** Changing the buying price changes yield, not the declared dividend per share.

### Face value and rate → dividend per share

**Recognition:** Income from ONE share

**Method:** d = Fr/100

**Common error:** Do not multiply by investment or use the market value.
