# Before you begin: rebuild the small skills

You do not need to know anything about the stock market to start. You do need percentages, the unitary method and simple equations. This page teaches those prerequisites before chapter questions begin.

Use paper. Try each checkpoint before opening its answer. If a skill is already comfortable, move on; if it is not, pause here rather than memorising share formulae on top of a gap.

## 1. A percentage always needs a base

“8%” means 8 out of every 100. **8% of what?** must be answered before calculating.

To find 8% of ₹50:

$$\frac{8}{100}\times50=\frac{400}{100}=₹4.$$

To find 8% of ₹75:

$$\frac{8}{100}\times75=₹6.$$

The same percentage gives different rupee amounts because the base changes. Later, **dividend percentage uses face value** while **yield uses money invested**.

Decimal percentages work in exactly the same way:

$$7.5\%=\frac{7.5}{100}=0.075,\qquad7.5\%\text{ of }₹80=0.075\times80=₹6.$$

Do not treat 7.5% as 0.75. That is 75%, ten times as large.

**Checkpoint F1:** Find 12% of ₹50 and 7.5% of ₹120.

<details>
<summary>Check F1</summary>

12/100 × 50 = **₹6**. 7.5/100 × 120 = **₹9**. In each case, identify the base first, then multiply by the percentage fraction.

</details>

## 2. Find what percentage one amount is of another

If you earn ₹8 after spending ₹80, the income per rupee spent is 8/80 = 0.1. To express this per ₹100, multiply by 100:

$$\text{Income percentage}=\frac{8}{80}\times100=10\%.$$

**The denominator names the thing you are comparing against.** “Income as a percentage of investment” puts investment below the line.

If the investment were ₹100 instead, the same ₹8 income would be 8%. Income in rupees and income percentage are not interchangeable.

**Checkpoint F2:** ₹300 income comes from ₹5,000 invested. Find the income percentage.

<details>
<summary>Check F2</summary>

Income percentage = 300/5000 × 100 = **6%**. Check: 6% of ₹5,000 is ₹300. Using 5000/300 would answer a different question.

</details>

## 3. The unitary method: price of one, price of many

One notebook costs ₹30. Eight notebooks cost 8 × 30 = ₹240.

Conversely, ₹240 spent at ₹30 per notebook buys 240/30 = 8 notebooks.

Shares use the same reasoning:

| Given | Wanted | Operation | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number and price of one | Total cost | Multiply | Repeat the one-item cost |
| Total cost and price of one | Number | Divide | Count how many one-item prices fit |
| Total cost and number | Price of one | Divide | Share the total cost equally |

**Checkpoint F3:** Each item costs ₹62.50. How many can ₹12,500 buy?

<details>
<summary>Check F3</summary>

Number = 12500/62.50 = **200**. Check: 62.50 × 2 = 125, so 62.50 × 200 = 12,500. The answer is a count, not rupees.

</details>

## 4. Reverse a calculation by undoing its operation

If 6n = 480, n is multiplied by 6. Divide **both sides** by 6:

$$n=\frac{480}{6}=80.$$

If x/12 = 450, x is divided by 12. Multiply both sides by 12:

$$x=450\times12=5400.$$

This is the basis of reverse questions. If every share earns ₹6 and total income is ₹480, the share count must be 480/6 = 80.

**Checkpoint F4:** Solve 7.5n = 600 and 0.08x = 400.

<details>
<summary>Check F4</summary>

n = 600/7.5 = **80**. x = 400/.08 = **5,000**. Substitution checks them: 7.5 × 80 = 600 and .08 × 5000 = 400.

</details>

## 5. An increase, a decrease and a remaining amount

₹50 increased by 20% becomes 50 + 20% of 50 = 50 + 10 = ₹60. The multiplier is **1.20**.

₹50 decreased by 20% becomes 50 − 10 = ₹40. The multiplier is **0.80**.

But ₹50 increased by **₹20** becomes ₹70. A rupee change and a percentage change are different instructions.

Similarly, “sell 80% of 120 shares” means sell 96 and retain 24. The remaining fraction is 100% − 80% = 20%.

**Checkpoint F5:** Find 25% below ₹80; then find how many of 150 shares remain if 40% are sold.

<details>
<summary>Check F5</summary>

25% of ₹80 = ₹20, so the new amount is **₹60**. 60% remain: .60 × 150 = **90 shares**. Check the sold count: 60 sold + 90 retained = 150.

</details>

## 6. Translate a sentence into one equation

“Divide ₹30,000 into two parts” means that if the first part is x, the second is **30000 − x**.

If the parts earn 10% and 15%, their incomes are .10x and .15(30000 − x). “Their incomes are equal” becomes:

$$.10x=.15(30000-x).$$

Now solve one step at a time:

$$.10x=4500-.15x$$
$$.10x+.15x=4500$$
$$.25x=4500$$
$$x=18000.$$

The other part is 30000 − 18000 = **₹12,000**. Check the condition: 10% of ₹18,000 and 15% of ₹12,000 are both ₹1,800.

**Checkpoint F6:** Equal amounts x earn 8% and 12%. The combined income is ₹1,000. Find x and the total investment.

<details>
<summary>Check F6</summary>

.08x + .12x = 1000, so .20x = 1000 and **x = ₹5,000**. This is the amount in **each** investment. Total = **₹10,000**. A correct x can still be an incomplete final answer.

</details>

## 7. Units and time periods

- Share count is a number of shares.
- Face value and market price are rupees **per share**.
- Investment and dividend are rupee amounts; annual dividend is per year.
- Dividend rate and yield are percentages, not rupees.
- “10% per half-year” gives two 10% payments in a year if the holding is unchanged. “10% per annum paid half-yearly” distributes one annual 10% amount across two payments.

**Checkpoint F7:** 50 shares of face value ₹100 pay 4% **each half-year**. What is the annual dividend?

<details>
<summary>Check F7</summary>

One share earns ₹4 in a half-year. Fifty earn ₹200 in one half-year. Two half-years give **₹400 annually**. Do not compound: reinvesting the dividend was not stated.

</details>

## 8. Two percentage changes do not simply cancel

An item with a ₹50 reference value is bought at 20% discount: cost = ₹40. It is then sold at 25% gain **on cost**: gain = 25% of ₹40 = ₹10, sale price = ₹50.

The gain is ₹10/₹40 × 100 = **25%**, not 25 − 20 = 5%. The percentages use different bases.

**Checkpoint F8:** Income rises from ₹800 to ₹1,000 on an original investment of ₹10,000. Find (a) relative income growth and (b) the additional income as a percentage of original investment.

<details>
<summary>Check F8</summary>

Increase = ₹200. (a) 200/800 × 100 = **25%**. (b) 200/10000 × 100 = **2%**. Same numerator, different question and denominator. The income/original-investment ratio rises from 8% to 10%, a **2 percentage-point** change.

</details>

You should be able to explain the base of a percentage, multiply/divide using one-item quantities, reverse a simple equation, and distinguish a total from an increase. Repair any missed foundation before its corresponding chapter skill.
